(SVPDP) that incorporates the handling cost incurred when rearranging the load at the customer locations.

1. Introduction
Single vehicle pickup and delivery problems
(SVPDPs) have received considerable attentionin recent years (see Berbeglia et al. 2007). Theseproblems consist of designing an optimal routefor a single vehicle making pickups and deliveriesto a set of customers. Here we are interested inone-to-many-to-one SVPDPs in which each customermay have a supply and a demand; all demandsoriginate from a depot and all supplies are destinedto the depot. A minimum cost circuit for which thevehicle load never exceeds the vehicle capacity hasto be determined. One-to-many-to-one SVPDPs haverecently been reviewed by Gribkovskaia and Laporte(2008), who have de´¼üned two variants: the SVPDPwith single demands (SVPDPÔÇôP/D), i.e., problemsin which customers require only a pickup or only adelivery, and the SVPDP with combined demands
(SVPDPÔÇôP&D), i.e., problems in which customersmayreceivecombinedpickupanddeliveryservices.
